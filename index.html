About

Computational approaches for electrochemical materials

I am a computational researcher specializing in electrocatalysis, electrochemical energy conversion, and next-generation energy storage. My work combines density functional theory (DFT), grand-canonical DFT, microkinetic modeling, electronic-structure analysis, and data-driven approaches to understand reaction mechanisms at electrified interfaces.

My research spans oxygen and chlorine evolution, nitrate and CO₂ reduction, and advanced battery chemistries including Li–S, Na–S, and metal–CO₂ systems.

Research

Two interconnected research directions

Energy Conversion

OER • CER • NO₃RR • CO₂RR

I investigate electrochemical reaction mechanisms at electrified interfaces, with emphasis on potential-dependent energetics, surface coverage, catalytic activity, selectivity, and reaction kinetics.

These studies combine DFT, grand-canonical DFT, computational hydrogen electrode models, electronic-structure analysis, and microkinetic modeling.

Explore Energy Conversion →

Energy Storage

Li–S • Na–S • Mg–CO₂ • Redox Mediators

I study next-generation batteries involving multistep conversion reactions, focusing on electrode materials, molecular mediators, catalytic interfaces, reaction reversibility, and discharge chemistry.

First-principles calculations and data-driven methods are used to establish molecular-level design principles for advanced energy-storage systems.
