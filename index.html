About Me

I am a computational researcher specializing in electrocatalysis, electrochemical energy conversion, and next-generation energy storage. My work combines density functional theory (DFT), grand-canonical DFT, microkinetic modeling, and data-driven approaches to understand reaction mechanisms at electrified interfaces and identify design principles for improved catalytic materials.

My research spans oxygen and chlorine evolution, nitrate and CO₂ reduction, and advanced battery chemistries including Li–S, Na–S, and metal–CO₂ systems.

Research

Energy Conversion

OER | CER | NO₃RR | CO₂ Reduction

My research in electrochemical energy conversion focuses on understanding reaction mechanisms at electrified interfaces and establishing relationships between catalyst structure, electrochemical potential, surface coverage, and catalytic performance.

I use DFT, grand-canonical DFT, computational hydrogen electrode models, electronic-structure analysis, and microkinetic modeling to investigate activity, selectivity, reaction pathways, and experimentally measurable quantities such as Tafel slopes.

Energy Storage

Li–S | Na–S | Mg–CO₂ | Redox Mediators

My research in electrochemical energy storage focuses on next-generation battery chemistries involving complex multistep conversion reactions.

Using first-principles calculations and quantum-chemistry approaches, I investigate electrode materials, molecular redox mediators, catalyst surfaces, and electrode–electrolyte interfaces that control reaction kinetics, reversibility, and intermediate stabilization.
